Modernist reference and design notes

Edward McKnight Kauffer, Drawing, 1934 · V&A, E.3770-2004

Observed and translated
A strong diagonal field and simplified geometric figure inform the directional blue rule; a tight grid and direct type hierarchy keep the product and action easy to scan.
Intended audience effect · hypothesis
Familiar everyday clothing may feel deliberately elevated without becoming formal or exclusive.
Risk
The strict grid could feel impersonal; approachable language and generous spacing counter that risk.

001

Postmodernist reference and design notes

Ron Arad, Concrete Stereo, 1983 · V&A, W.7-2011

Observed and translated
The unexpected meeting of audio equipment and rough concrete, with visible components, inspires layered planes, mixed typography, and a deliberately off-center blue form.
Intended audience effect · hypothesis
The familiar tee may read as an expressive everyday choice, not a status uniform.
Risk
Layering can compete with the action; the headline and button stay on a clear, high-contrast field.

These are live comparison studies, not a selected direction or a claim about customer response. The white T-shirt remains the same product in both.
